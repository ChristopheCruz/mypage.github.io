    HiGeoMes¶

    Research and goals¶

    Full project title: The historical geography of Upper Mesopotamia in the 2nd millennium BC: an interdisciplinary research project

    Project start and duration: February 2011 - 36 months

    Project reference: ANR-10-FRAL-0003

    Summary¶

    Because of its geostrategic position and the varied conditions of its natural environment, Upper Mesopotamia held an important place in the history of the ancient Near East. The concrete physical parameters of the region influenced the forms of its socio-economic appropriation and political occupation. The recent growth of archaeological and epigraphic data and the collection of natural history information now make it possible to establish a historical geography of this region on solid foundations. The goal of HIGEOMES is to integrate this information, processed by different disciplines, taking the 2nd millennium BC as a chronological sample. Over the long span of a millennium, we seek to analyse the interaction between the natural environment, patterns of land occupation and communication routes. Studying the geographical transformations of Upper Mesopotamia under different political regimes will highlight continuities and breaks in access to the region's resources. Archaeological and written evidence and palaeo-environmental data are not only assessed in themselves, but also processed with a computer model coupled with thematic surveys. The period was chosen for its historical and cultural diversity, reflected in the heterogeneity of the sources. Research, though very dynamic, is currently characterised by strictly disciplinary approaches. The main feature of the proposed cooperation is the combination of complementary skills of the French and German partners: this concerns not only the correlation between archaeology, Assyriology and geo-informatic modelling, but also thematic and methodological specialisations within each discipline. HIGEOMES builds on earlier cooperation between the partners, which established the methodological foundations and questions at the centre of this project.

    On the computer science side, this project is based, for the Old Babylonian period, on the ArchiBab database. It references texts, more or less complete, found on tablets during archaeological excavations. Our goal is to represent the information in this database clearly and to bring out certain aspects, such as the links between taking a given route to reach a given village because of an environmental constraint. One aim, among others, could be to locate toponyms whose coordinates are still unknown.
